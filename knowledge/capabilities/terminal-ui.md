# Terminal UI

`soul-sever` with no arguments enters the alternate screen and enables mouse capture. An empty username draws the sign-in form: username, masked password, the server from the config file, and the title-style lines `A name that is not already registered` and `creates a new Soulseek account.` The next line says an existing name signs in with that account's password. Enter on the password stores it in the system keyring, writes the username to the account file, and starts login. The form says the password is saved in the system keyring. Esc quits. No socket opens before that. A saved username skips the form. The header says `signing in` and the hints row says `signing in…` until `LoggedIn`. That login clears the hints row and replaces the header with the account and banner. The lists stay empty until login finishes. `App::preview()` still loads `preview-` rows for tests. The binary does not call it.

After `App::apply_session(LoggedIn)`, the header shows the account and banner. A later `Listening` event keeps the banner and adds the interface address, such as `10.8.0.5`, beside the server. A specific bind shows that address. An empty bind shows the local address of the connection to the server. A listen failure clears it. A `PeerFailed` event keeps the login and sets the notice to `username: message`. `LoginFailed` and `TimedOut` return to the sign-in form, prefilled with the account name, and show the reason in the header and the hints row. A disconnect before login does the same. `Kicked` replaces the banner with `logged in elsewhere` and does not open the form. Live meter samples are the new completed transfer bytes on each 200 ms tick, scaled to one second. Before login the meters stay at zero. Those totals are updated while the transfer is still running, about every 200 ms, so the chart and the progress rail move across the transfer. The speed column shows that interval's bytes per second and is blank once the transfer leaves `Transferring`. The download and upload charts draw those samples spread over about a second, so one fast file is a hill. The peak figure under the chart stays the unsmoothed maximum. Share counts come from the session's `Shares` event. On Shares, `j` and `k` pick public, buddy, or trusted. Enter opens a folder browser at the home directory. Enter opens a directory, `s` shares the folder you are in, and Esc closes the browser. `[` and `]` mark a listed path, and `x` removes it. A path list longer than the column keeps the marked path on screen. A missing directory does not open. The settings shares section lists those paths. Press `9` for settings. `j` and `k` pick a section. `[` and `]` pick a field. A section longer than the pane keeps the selected field on screen. Enter edits it, and `x` clears a limit, a folder, or a list. Enter on bind address opens a popup of this machine's interfaces. `all interfaces` leaves the address empty. Choosing an interface saves that address. Upload slots, speed limits, download folders, search limits, and chat options apply to the running session. Account, server, listen port, bind address, and UPnP save and ask for a restart. Share folders stay on the shares view.

Mouse capture is enabled for the session and disabled on exit. A left click selects the widget under the pointer: numbered tabs, table rows, share panels, the search query line, the search mode line, and the `?` help control. Dragging across rows moves the selection. Dragging a panel edge grows that panel outward — east, west, south, or north — and dragging back stops at the panel's original size. On chat, the rooms panel starts at width 24. Dragging its east edge widens the room list, and dragging that edge back west stops at 24. The transcript's west edge widens the text by taking columns from the rooms panel, and dragging that edge back restores the rooms panel to 24. The member list, search query, search filters, users detail, settings sections, share columns, and dashboard meters follow the same rule. The sizes last for the session and are not written to the config file. A list longer than its pane draws a vertical track in the right padding column: `│` for the track and `█` for the thumb. The thumb's length is the visible fraction of the list, at least one row, and its position is the first visible row. A list that fits draws no track. Dragging the thumb moves that window and leaves the highlighted row where it is. A press on the track outside the thumb jumps one pane toward the press and also leaves the highlight. `j` and `k` move the selection one row. The window stays put until the highlight reaches the top or bottom edge, then it follows. The wheel and the thumb move the window and do not change the selection. They apply to the surface under the pointer: the main list, the room list, the in-room list, a library column, share paths, settings fields, the folder browser, the bind-address popup, the transcript, and the log. The transcript no longer moves the room highlight. Changing rooms returns the member list to the top and the transcript to the newest lines. A new search returns the results list to the top. A click while help is open closes help.

Key `a` opens the quality view, except on chat, where `a` still starts a message. Key `r` on that view forgets every saved verdict and measures the library again. The notice is `rechecking quality`. The hint says `r checks again`, and help lists `check quality again`. The tab reads `spec`. The panel above the columns counts songs being measured and songs that need a replacement, then the highlighted row. The columns are measuring and replacing. A song in analysis says `measuring`, `waiting`, or `playing`. A song that needs a better file says `needs a lossless copy` or `needs a 24-bit copy`. The one being fetched names the advertised quality when the peer sent it, then what is happening: `starting the download`, `downloading N%`, `in queue`, or `checking the new file`. The highlighted row says what this library file is (`have …`), and for a download, who it is coming from and the remote file name. Search hits are not listed. Several replacements download at once, and each user sends only one. A song whose only copies are from busy users says `waiting for a free user`. An empty column says `idle`. Opening the view and moving the highlight read a cached board. The catalog file is written when a measurement batch finishes, not as each file is measured. `[` and `]` change column. `j` and `k` move inside the column. A click selects a row and does not start playback. While a song is playing, `frame_wait` stays 33 ms. This view itself stays on the 200 ms tick.

`tab` / `h` / `l` cycle views. `j` and `k` move the selection. The wheel moves the list under the pointer. On the library, `[` and `]` change column and Enter plays the song. The spectrum meter is documented in [Music library and playback](library.md). On Search, typing fills the query, and `/` or a click does the same. `m` or a click on the mode line cycles global, room, buddy, user, and wishlist. A click on a filter row changes it. `]` cycles quality through any, lossless, and lossy. Bitrate, duration, free slot, file type, and size cycle the same way. Country, include, and exclude take typed text. A lossless file stays visible under a bitrate floor. An exclude token hides a hit whose user or path contains it. The test fixture catalog is also filtered by the query text. After login the catalog starts empty, Enter sends the query, the notice is `searching for` that text, and a `SearchResult` for that search's token is shown, including a path that does not contain the query phrase. A later search closes the previous token, so a late reply from the earlier query stays off the list. A folder reply is added only while that album is still requested, and only files in that directory are kept. A nested directory in the same reply is left off the list. Search rows put every album above single songs. Within albums, and then within songs, a shorter upload queue comes first. At the same queue length a free slot comes first, then a lossless file before a lossy one. The flat hit list stays lossless, then other files, then lossy. FLAC, WAV, AIFF, ALAC, APE, WavPack, TAK, TTA, Shorten, DSD, PCM, and CAF stay above MP3, AAC, Ogg, Opus, WMA, and the other lossy extensions. `.m4a` is lossy unless the result includes a bit depth. Order inside a group follows arrival. Two or more files that share a directory are one album row (`▸` closed, `▾` open) until a click or Enter on that row. Dragging across the results moves the highlight and leaves the album open or closed. Once results stop arriving, those grouped rows stay cached, and a frame draws only the rows that fit on screen. Keys and mouse events already waiting are handled before the next draw. A lone file stays one row. `d` on an album queues every file in it and asks the peer for `FolderContents`. Songs that come back and were not already listed are queued too. Enter on a file still sends the search. When not logged in, Enter stores the notice `not logged in`. Before login the lists are empty. On Users, `n`, `p`, and `t` toggle notify, priority, and trusted for a saved buddy and write the config file. Enter on Users or Browse, when logged in, asks that user for a share list. `x` bans and `i` ignores the selected user or search hit. A preview username is refused. On Chat, letters go into the message under the room. A draft longer than the transcript's inner width continues on the next row, and the cursor block stays on the last of those rows. An empty field stays the single row `type a message`. Enter sends the line in the selected room when logged in. Esc leaves the line and leaves the draft visible. Arrow keys still move the room list. Rooms are ordered by participant count, largest first, and rooms with the same count are alphabetical. The highlighted room stays highlighted when that order changes. A click on the message line focuses it. `/` focuses it without inserting a slash. `j`, `k`, `h`, `l`, and `q` are letters on this view. Digits still switch views, and `ctrl-c` still quits. Enter or a click on a room in the list, when logged in, sends `JoinRoom` for that room. The in-room list is alphabetical by name. The highlighted person stays highlighted when that list changes. A click on a name in the in-room list highlights that person and opens a card. The card shows status, country, and file counts when the join reply included them, then browse files, add friend, ignore, ban, and close. Opening the card asks the server for that user's status, stats, and interests. Browse files switches to Browse and asks for their share list. Add friend writes the name into the config buddies and the users list. `[` returns the keys to the room list and `]` moves them to the people list. After login the transcript and member list start empty. A live line is the only line for that room. A room line and the ticker wrap to the transcript's inner width. The time and username stay on the first row, and later rows hold the rest of the body. The newest visual rows stay at the bottom until the wheel or the thumb moves up. New lines while that window is at the bottom stay at the bottom. New lines while reading earlier rows leave the reader where they were. A line break in the body starts a new row. Resizing the terminal or dragging the transcript edge reflows those same lines. The ticker string is `ticker · —` until a ticker arrives. `auto_away_minutes` of 1 queues away on the 300th 200 ms tick and a later key queues online; 0 minutes never queues away. A port-map result sets the notice (`port mapped` or `port map failed`) and leaves the login banner in place. The settings screen still shows `upnp / nat-pmp` as `off`. `d` on Search, when logged in, queues a download of the selected visible hit. `x` on Downloads, Uploads, or the dashboard removes the highlighted row. `X` on Downloads clears finished downloads, on Uploads clears finished uploads, and on the dashboard clears both. The notice is `cleared finished`. The downloads and uploads views say `x removes the row` and `X clears finished`. A preview row is dropped locally. A live row is also cancelled in the session, and the notice is `removed`. The results title and the notice row both say `d downloads this album` or `d downloads this song` while the result count is showing. Help says `download the highlighted`. A preview username is refused with notice `preview row`. Not logged in, the notice is `not logged in`. Download and upload tables are titled `download queue` and `upload queue`. The outgoing header and the dashboard network line show uploads in progress over the configured upload slots, such as `1/5` when one upload is transferring and upload slots is 5. Progress in those tables, and on the dashboard queue, is a ten-cell braille rail: `⣿` filled, `⣇` when the last cell is only half full, `⣀` for the rest, then the percent. Downloads use the green meter ramp and uploads the blue one. Before login the meters stay at zero. `?` or a click on the help control opens help. The help panel draws a block-letter `soul sever` wordmark, then `-by E.N. Ramos <enramos@live.com>-` flush with the right edge of that art, then the command list. `q` quits, except while help is open, where it closes help. `ctrl-c` always quits. A click anywhere while help is open also closes help.

On the library view the log is not drawn. That pane is two rows: the seek bar, then icon-sized `▶`, `❚❚`, and `■`. A click starts sound after a short decode, not after the whole file. Filing a finished download runs on its own thread, so the download meter keeps ticking while the file is renamed. The library list updates when that scan finishes. While a song is playing, that same finish does not stop the playback meter. While a song is playing the event loop redraws about every 33 ms. The 200 ms tick still updates transfer rates and idle time. On every other view, a log sits under the view and above the tabs. It opens with the session. `` ` `` or a click on its title hides it down to one line, `▸ log`, which keeps the latest message. Opening it again shows the stored lines, newest at the bottom, each prefixed with `HH:MM:SS`, until the wheel or the thumb moves up. New log lines while that window is at the bottom stay at the bottom. An empty log says `no messages`. Help lists `` ` `` as show or hide the log. A peer browse is `User bob is browsing your list of shared files`. A search of our files is `User bob is searching for "notes", found 1 results`. The same pane records `Listening on port`, a finished upload or download, a disconnect, a kick, a peer failure, and a failed share scan. The unsigned-in shell does not invent those lines.
